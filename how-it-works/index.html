How It Works

A repipe designed around the building.

Plumbing Track is more than a way to replace failing pipe. The project is planned around the building, the people living in it, and the finished system that remains afterward.

The system

First, choose where the replacement plumbing will live.

Plumbing Track uses an enclosed, accessible route rather than automatically returning new pipe to concealed walls and ceilings. That routing decision shapes how the rest of the project is planned.

Accessible does not mean exposed. The new pipe is enclosed within the Plumbing Track system and finished as part of the building.

Project process

Assessment through handoff.

The project sequence is designed around occupied multifamily buildings. Assessment, resident coordination, installation, finishing, and handoff are treated as one connected job rather than separate problems.

01

Assessment & Proposal

Understand the building, existing pipe, constraints, occupancy, and the route that will support the replacement system.

02

Prepare the building.

Coordinate access, communication, protection, staging, and the practical details required to work in occupied homes.

03

Install Around Occupied Homes

Install the replacement system through the planned route while keeping the work focused on the spaces that actually require access.

04

Finish the Building

Complete the architectural treatment and leave each work area as a finished part of the building, not an ongoing construction zone.

05

Handoff With Future Access

Close the project with a known enclosed route that remains serviceable when the plumbing needs attention later.

Occupied-building coordination

The process is more than the pipe install.

In an occupied property, the schedule only works when resident communication, suite access, protection, staging, and finishing are built into the same sequence.

Before work

Residents know what to expect.

Communication, suite access, protection, and practical preparation are established before crews begin the in-suite work.

During work

Keep each visit focused.

Staging and route planning keep the crew focused on the areas that need access instead of turning the entire home into the work area.

At handoff

Finish the room and retain the route.

Closeout leaves a finished route.
