How It Works

A repipe designed around the building.

Plumbing Track is more than a way to replace failing pipe. The route, the installation method, and future access are planned as part of the finished plumbing system.

Accessible plumbing

The route is part of how the system works.

Conventional repiping can solve the immediate material problem while placing the replacement pipe back behind finished surfaces. Plumbing Track plans an enclosed, accessible route so inspection and future service do not automatically begin with opening the building again.

Accessible does not mean exposed. The pipe is enclosed within the Plumbing Track system; the difference is that access is deliberately designed into the finished building.

Conventional repipe

Replace the pipe, then close the building around it.

Routing is constrained by walls, ceilings, and cavities. Future inspection or service can require reopening finished surfaces.

Plumbing Track

Plan the route and keep service access in the system.

The new pipe follows a deliberate enclosed route intended to reduce unnecessary demolition during installation and make future access more straightforward.

Project process

From assessment to a finished, accessible system.

The project sequence is designed around occupied multifamily buildings. The point is not simply to get new pipe into the building, but to manage the work, the residents, the finished condition, and the system that remains afterward.

01

Assessment & Proposal

Understand the building, existing pipe, constraints, occupancy, and the route that will support the replacement system.

02

Prepare the Building & Residents

Coordinate access, communication, protection, staging, and the practical details required to work in occupied homes.

03

Install Around Occupied Homes

Install the replacement system using the planned accessible route while limiting the amount of finished building that needs to be opened.

04

Finish the Building

Complete the visible architectural treatment and leave the property as a finished building rather than an ongoing construction zone.

05

Keep Future Access

The project ends with a plumbing system designed so future inspection and service can reach the route without repeating the original demolition.

Why this matters

Installation day is only part of the decision.

The accessible-plumbing idea connects the installation method to the life of the building afterward. Reduced opening during the repipe is useful now; deliberate access is useful the next time the system needs attention.

During the project

Less of the building needs to become the work area.

Planned routing is intended to reduce unnecessary wall and ceiling disturbance compared with repeatedly chasing concealed pipe.

At completion

The track is part of the finished architecture.

The replacement route is enclosed and deliberately integrated into the building rather than left as exposed piping.

Years later

Access remains part of what the building owns.

Future service can begin at the accessible system instead of automatically beginning with demolition of finished surfaces.
