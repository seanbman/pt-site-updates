The Case for Accessible Plumbing

Pipes eventually fail. What makes a failure extraordinarily expensive is where the system is located and when the building discovers the problem.

The central idea

Every pipe in a wall is a pipe you can’t inspect.

Conventional replacement can solve the immediate material problem while placing the new pipe back behind finished surfaces. Plumbing Track plans an enclosed route where access is deliberately part of the finished system.

Accessible does not mean exposed. The pipe is enclosed within the track; the difference is that service access is designed into the building rather than left to the next demolition project.

01 / Discover

The expensive failures are the late ones.

Accessibility supports earlier discovery and more straightforward service. Plumbing Track does not claim to prevent pipe failure.

02 / Install

Work done in the open is better work.

A planned visible run makes routing, joints, and support spacing easier to see during installation than work constrained inside a cavity.

03 / Own

You get to choose the route.

The route can be designed around the building, its occupied homes, and the finished condition that residents live with.

The finished system

Fewer holes in the building. More thought in the route.

Plumbing Track is an enclosed architectural system, not exposed pipe. Planned routing is intended to reduce unnecessary openings while allowing required fire-separation crossings to be properly detailed.
